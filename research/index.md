# Research

We study the foundations of learning and decision-making, connecting machine learning with optimization, game theory, and control.

## AI evaluation and alignment

At [Princeton AI Alignment and Safety (AIAS)](https://alignment.dais.princeton.edu/), we develop methods to evaluate capabilities beyond human expertise and align AI behavior through incentives and interaction.

- **[Measuring Intelligence Beyond Human Scale (SepaRank)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07040):** model-generated challenges for relative evaluation without human-authored benchmarks or a semantic judge.
- **[AI Alignment via Incentives and Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01643):** a mechanism-design approach that adapts rewards for solver–auditor interactions using bandit feedback.

## Online learning and optimization

Our work develops efficient algorithms for regret minimization, adaptive optimization, and decision-making under uncertainty.

- **[AdaGrad](https://jmlr.org/papers/v12/duchi11a.html)** and adaptive regularization: adapting optimization updates to the geometry of past gradients.
- **[Online Newton Step](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-007-5016-8):** logarithmic regret for exp-concave losses through second-order online optimization.
- **Sublinear-time optimization** for [linear classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4408) and [semidefinite programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5211).
- **Projection-free optimization:** [online algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.4657) and [linear convergence for smooth, strongly convex objectives over polyhedral sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.4666).
- **[Matrix prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0136)** and [continuous multi-armed bandits](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6191328).

For background, see [Introduction to Online Convex Optimization](https://sites.google.com/view/intro-oco/) and [Optimization for Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03550).

## Control and sequence modeling

We develop online nonstochastic control and spectral methods for learning dynamical systems and predicting long sequences. Our sequence-modeling work includes collaborations with [Google DeepMind Princeton](https://sites.google.com/view/gbrainprinceton/home).

- **[Online nonstochastic control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09619):** learning control policies under adversarial disturbances and changing costs. The framework includes [gradient perturbation controllers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08721), [logarithmic regret](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05062), [unknown dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12178), and [partial observation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09254).
- **[Universal Sequence Preconditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06545)** and its [second-order extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08390): memory-efficient prediction using orthogonal polynomials.
- **[Universal Learning of Nonlinear Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11990):** provable prediction through observation-based spectral filtering, building on [spectral filtering for linear dynamical systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00946) and [asymmetric systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03981).
- **[Spectral Transformers](https://sites.google.com/view/gbrainprinceton/projects/spectral-transformers)** and [FutureFill](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03766): efficient neural sequence architectures and fast generation for convolutional models.
- **Learning and control:** [boosting for dynamical systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08720), [maximum-entropy exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02690), [bandit feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05523), [black-box control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06650), and [adaptive control of time-varying dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04393).
